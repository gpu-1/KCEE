    Understanding stress and energy checks

    Energy vs half load work

    Strain energy is the elastic energy stored in the deformed bracket. Applied-load work is calculated from the recorded forces and their corresponding displacements. For this static, linear-elastic model with zero prescribed support displacement, strain energy should equal half the final force–displacement sum. A pass means these two calculations agree within the 0.1% demo tolerance. It does not prove that the support, material or load represents a real assembly.

    Energy change with mesh refinement

    The last strain-energy change compares the two finest meshes. A small change suggests the total stored energy is becoming less sensitive to mesh size. There is no acceptance threshold for this value. A local stress peak may still change substantially while total energy and displacement remain nearly stable.

    Stress peaks and colors

    The peak in the mesh table comes from stresses at solver integration points. The stress colors come from extrapolated and averaged nodal values, so their maximum can differ. Sharp transitions, idealized supports and load boundaries can produce highly sensitive local peaks. The current stress peak is unconverged and must not be used as an accepted allowable or factor-of-safety result.

    Regional stress and element quality

    Regional peaks exclude elements whose corner centroid is close to the nominal clamp or load rectangles. This helps investigate where peaks occur; it does not remove the global peak or establish a validated stress limit. Corner quality describes the straight tetrahedron formed by the element’s four corner nodes. It does not check the full curved quadratic element.

    Engineering acceptance still requires justified mounting and loads, suitable material allowables, stress convergence and physical validation.

    Baseline mesh vs Native PicoGK

    Baseline mesh and PicoGK are two ways to generate the bracket’s geometry. They are separate from the structural solver.

    Baseline mesh

    A simple L bracket with sharp corners. Python writes triangles directly into an STL file. It is a fixed, minimal demonstration shape and runs without the native geometry service. Its current analysis uses simple analytical screening.

    Native PicoGK

    An L bracket with four mounting holes and a rounded root transition. PicoGK constructs voxel geometry, then exports an STL. The detail is limited by voxel resolution, and generation requires the native PicoGK service on this Mac.

    It starts with analytical screening. Select Analyze selected bracket to run Gmsh/CalculiX finite-element analysis on the actual PicoGK shape.

    Geometry is separate from analysis

    Both options initially use the same ideal-cantilever formulas to select thickness. Choosing PicoGK alone does not make those formulas account for holes or the bend. The later finite-element analysis uses the actual shape.

    Which should I use?

    Use Baseline mesh to check that the basic workflow works. Use Native PicoGK for the detailed bracket and its current stress/displacement study.

    “Baseline mesh” means the triangle surface used to display the shape. The finite-element volume mesh used by CalculiX is generated separately.
